06 / Robotic systems

Autonomous aquatic robot.

Mechanical design of an extendable vegetation cutter and drivetrain interfaces for an aquatic platform.

MachiningCADRobotics

Overview

Developed mechanical interfaces for a vegetation removal robot, including an extendable cutter and a two-piece threaded motor coupler with a keyed interface. Components were fabricated using milling and turning processes.

Mechanical requirements

In the Collective Embodiment Intelligence Lab, I worked on the mechanical interface between the robot chassis and an underwater vegetation-cutting assembly. The mechanism was designed to extend the cutter from the surface to approximately 1 m depth.

Motor coupling and fabrication

I designed a two-piece threaded motor coupler with a keyed interface to transfer rotation between the motor and cutting assembly. The separable design supports assembly and servicing. I fabricated the components using a programmable mill and lathe, translating CAD geometry into machinable parts.

Design considerations

The mechanical work required attention to shaft alignment, torque transfer, component fit, assembly access and the integration of the extendable mechanism with the chassis. My contribution focused on the mechanical subsystem rather than the robot’s autonomous navigation software.
