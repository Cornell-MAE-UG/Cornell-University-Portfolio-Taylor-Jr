Overview

I developed and tested embedded software, wireless communication, sensing and feedback control for a dynamic autonomous mobile robot in Cornell’s graduate Fast Robots course.

Embedded systems and wireless communication

In MAE 5190 (Spring 2026), I worked with the Artemis Nano, Arduino firmware and Python/Jupyter interfaces. Early labs established Bluetooth Low Energy command handling, timestamped data transfer and sensor acquisition. For the graduate-level extension, I developed a musical note tuner targeting C6, E6 and G6 using microphone input.

Sensing and motor integration

I integrated inertial and time-of-flight sensing with a mobile robot, configured ranging sensors and developed PWM motor commands for forward, reverse and turning motion. Open-loop experiments informed motor calibration and subsequent feedback control.

Feedback control

I implemented distance control toward a 12-inch wall setpoint, using PID tuning, a deadband and integral limiting. For orientation control I converted IMU DMP quaternion output to yaw, calibrated the heading offset and tested PD gains. The documented orientation controller used Kp = 3.75, Kd = 40 and a 2° deadband.

Dynamic maneuvers and localization

For the drift maneuver I developed a state machine to approach a wall, execute a 180° turn and drive away. I adjusted the trigger distance from 12 to 18 inches to provide turning space; the final maneuver did not rely on the unreliable Kalman-filter implementation. Later work explored grid-based Bayes localization with a 12 × 9 × 18 pose grid and 18 sensor observations.
