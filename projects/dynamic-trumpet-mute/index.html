Dynamic trumpet mute.

Exploring adjustable acoustic attenuation while minimizing playing resistance.

AcousticsProduct designPrototyping

Overview

The project investigates how mute geometry and adjustable acoustic pathways can balance sound reduction with the resistance experienced by a trumpet player. This is ongoing work; performance results are not yet presented.

Design motivation

Practice mutes allow musicians to rehearse at lower sound levels, but restricting the acoustic path can change the instrument’s playing resistance and response. My M.Eng. project investigates whether a dynamically adjustable mute can offer more control over that compromise.

Engineering approach

I am exploring the relationship between internal volume, outlet geometry and acoustic attenuation. The design problem is to reduce radiated sound without introducing excessive airflow resistance or disrupting the instrument’s response. Candidate approaches include changing the acoustic path, incorporating absorptive materials and using adjustable openings.

Proposed validation

I plan to compare candidate geometries against an unmuted trumpet and a conventional practice mute using repeatable notes, microphone measurements and an assessment of playing resistance. Sound-pressure reduction, frequency response and repeatability are proposed evaluation criteria, not measured results.

Current status

This is ongoing graduate research. Prototype photographs, CAD iterations and measured acoustic performance will be added once available.
